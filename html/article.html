Cities rethink public space for a changing climate

From shaded streets to restored waterfronts, a quiet transformation is changing how millions of people experience urban life.

BY AMARA REED 24 JUN 2025 8 MIN READ 42 COMMENTS
SHARE

The city of the near future may look surprisingly familiar. It will still have busy streets, corner shops and crowded stations. What changes is the space between them—and who that space is designed to serve.

Across the world, planners are revisiting assumptions that shaped cities for much of the last century. Roads once treated primarily as channels for traffic are becoming places to pause. Vacant lots are turning into gardens. Rivers hidden behind concrete are being brought back into view.

A different measure of progress

The most ambitious projects are not always the largest. In many neighborhoods, a few trees, a safe crossing and a well-placed bench can alter the rhythm of an entire street.

“The best public spaces don’t tell people what to do. They make more kinds of life possible.”

That flexibility matters as extreme heat and heavier rainfall become part of everyday planning. Trees cool pavements, planted areas absorb water and car-free routes give emergency networks more resilience.

Designed with, not for

Perhaps the most important shift is procedural. Residents are being invited into the process earlier, before the major decisions are fixed. Their observations often reveal what maps and models miss.

The result is a more patient kind of urbanism—one that treats the city not as a finished object, but as a living agreement between the people who share it.
